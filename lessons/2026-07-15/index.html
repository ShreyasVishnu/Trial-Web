Stop polling for frames.

Your model is already throttled and downscaled. Today you remove work around the model without changing its accuracy settings.

Why this matters. MediaPipe's Web detectForVideo() call is synchronous and blocks the main thread. Every extra layout read, canvas copy, and DOM write in that same loop competes with input and drawing.

What the audit found

  • renderFrame() is driven by requestAnimationFrame, then polls video.currentTime to learn whether a camera frame exists (lines 1437–1541).
  • resizeCanvasToViewport() reads getBoundingClientRect() inside the frame loop (line 1460), even though metadata, resize, orientation, and visual-viewport handlers already resize it (lines 1652–1663).
  • The video is copied into the inference canvas on every camera frame (lines 1469–1470), while inference only runs every second frame (lines 1472–1475).
  • Latency is reduced and written to the DOM on every loop (lines 1451–1455); gesture and timer text are also updated per camera frame.
  • The visibility handler checks permissions when the page returns, but it does not explicitly pause the camera pipeline while hidden (lines 1658–1662).
  • The June 16 result was 30 render FPS, 44 ms inference, and 14 IPS after downscaling. That is historical context, not today's baseline.
Required change: make the camera drive the loop. Replace the animation-frame polling loop with HTMLVideoElement.requestVideoFrameCallback(). Schedule the next callback only while the camera is running, cancel it when the camera stops, and move canvas sizing out of the hot loop. If the API is unavailable, show which scheduler is active; do not silently change behavior.

Do not touch these knobs

Keep INFERENCE_SCALE, INFERENCE_EVERY_N_FRAMES, model, delegate, and confidence settings unchanged. You already tested those. This experiment isolates scheduling and housekeeping.
